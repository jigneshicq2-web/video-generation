**Hindi reels must sound like real Indian agency life (Dinesh 2026-09-26, Payment Pakka).** Translated English punchlines ("Advance mein", "Pehle payment, phir Diwali") didn't capture how Indian agencies actually talk. Agencies ask for money indirectly and politely ("wo pichhle mahine ka bill... thoda dekh lijiye na", "bas yaad dila rahi thi"), clients dodge with courtesy and sweets ("mithai ka dabba bhijwa raha hoon"), and the agency's real leverage is the subtle hint when the client needs them ("ji ji, main abhi dekh leti hoon. Wo zara ek payment ka... aap dekh lijiyega please"). Clients don't visit the office to say they can't pay; they say it on the phone. More generally, an Indian client never comes to meet the agency (Dinesh 2026-09-28, Voice Note draft 1: Gupta ji sitting in Priya's office after his voice note "doesn't make sense unless he has an ulterior motive to meet her"). All client contact in Hindi reels is a call, a WhatsApp message or a voice note; put Gupta ji in a corner card on the phone, not in her office. And the client never reaches out first (Dinesh 2026-09-28, Voice Note draft 2: "the client would never call an agency to tell them that he has feedback. The agency would fight for their attention, for their feedback, for their approval"). Priya chases politely; Gupta ji replies late, if at all. Ask Dinesh for the natural phrasing when unsure, and let a visual (a PAID stamp) finish the joke instead of an extra line.
